Task Board

A card shows the client, the work, who holds it, and one flag if something is wrong. Open a card to move it, tick a step, or answer the client.

Viewing as
The card, side by side

What came off

Today

Filled label chip, the word “Medium”, the client, the creator, an icon row — and one face.

Proposed

Client, title, the crew, the date. One flag when it is in trouble. Progress is a hairline.

The decisions

  1. Support faces on the card. A task has had one lead and many supporters since 2026-08-06 and the card printed one of them — “who is on this?” was a click away, on every card. The lead keeps the green ring; you keep a violet one.
  2. One flag, never two, and only when something is wrong: client-asked ▸ not-synced ▸ in-review ▸ changes ▸ urgent ▸ parked. Overdue is not in that list because the red date already says it. Priority is silent unless it is Urgent, and the discipline is a 6px dot — thirty cards reading “Medium” under a filled orange chip is thirty cards reading nothing.
  3. Three rows of chrome became one. The sort selector and the density toggle are gone rather than moved: a board has one sensible order — trouble first — and one comfortable size. Clear appears only when there is something to clear.
  4. The columns lost their walls. White cards inside white panels on a grey page is two surfaces doing one job; the panel is now the gap. Columns fold — five of them need ~1500px beside a 248px sidebar, which is why the board already scrolls sideways at 1800px.
  5. Moving a card no longer needs a mouse. Drag still works, but the record has a Move to control, and both go through one function — so the two doors into a stage cannot apply different rules, which is exactly how the live board ended up with cards sitting in the parked column that were never actually parked.
  6. The record opens with the crew, then four facts, one quiet notice if something is wrong, and Work / Comments / Activity as tabs — one screen instead of two scrolls.
  7. One board, three seats. The switcher runs the server's own predicates, so the employee board really is “my work — lead, support or any step I own — plus my team's unclaimed queue”, with no client cards, no priority control, no approvals and no delegation. Priority becomes a control instead of a value for a manager; a step someone else owns is disabled and says whose it is. Super admin and AM see the same board — that difference is off this page.

# Sentinel Task SOP — how work gets created, shaped and finished

**For:** everyone (COO · Account Managers · Team Leads · Specialists) · **From:** 2026-09-02 (the clean-board reset)
**One sentence:** every piece of client or internal work lives as **one card with one accountable lead**, created **AI-first** by the person who made the commitment, and finished **through review** — nothing else counts as done.

---

## 0. The five objects, in one minute

| Object | What it is | Who creates it |
|---|---|---|
| **Project** | A named OUTCOME with a date — "Phase One: a replicable pod by Oct 1". Holds milestones + tasks. | COO / AM (`projects.manage`) |
| **Milestone** | A checkable *statement of done* inside a project — "Report Standard v1 used in a live meeting". Not a task: nobody is assigned; someone senior ticks it when it is TRUE. | COO / AM |
| **Task (card)** | One person-sized deliverable — the unit everything else measures. | Anyone (AI-first for managers) |
| **Step (subtask)** | A phase or checklist line INSIDE a card's Work breakdown. Steps can each have an owner. | Whoever runs the card |
| **Recurring service** | A retainer deliverable that mints a fresh card every period (weekly report, monthly newsletter). | AM (`tasks.recurring`) |

Rule of thumb for which to use: **if it will take somebody a work session or more, it's a task. If it's a stage inside that task, it's a step. If it's true/false about the company, it's a milestone. If it repeats on a calendar, it's a recurring service.**

## 1. Creating tasks — the AI-first way (the default for AMs and leads)

You just left a client call, or agreed something in a meeting. **Do not open a blank form.** Instead:

1. Open the **Task Board** → press **✦ Plan with AI** (also on any project page, and inside a client's page).
2. Pick the **client** (or leave "No client — internal work").
3. Type what was agreed, *like you'd say it to a colleague*:
   > "We promised TCS the September Meta analysis before Thursday's meeting. Analysis first, then three evidence-backed findings added into the report."
4. Press **Draft tasks**. The AI answers with 1–5 proposed cards — each with a title, department, **suggested assignee**, due date, effort estimate, reviewer (when the person's stage needs one) and one sentence of *why*.
   - The suggestion is grounded in Sentinel's own facts: who already holds this client's work in that department, who is on leave, who is heavy, whose stage requires review. It **cannot invent a colleague**.
   - ⚠️ Warnings under a proposal (leave booked, already heavy, missing certification) are computed by Sentinel, not the AI — read them.
5. **Override anything**: edit the title, change the assignee dropdown, change the date, untick "keep" on a proposal you don't want.
6. Press **Create the kept tasks**. Only now does anything exist — with exactly the same rules as the manual form. A task that "waits on" another is parked automatically until the first is approved.

**If the AI is down**, the button says so and you use **New Task** — the form always works. The AI is an accelerator, never a gate.

### 1b. Creating tasks by hand (everyone)

**New Task** on the board, or "+ Add card" at the foot of a column:

- **Name** — for campaign work use the pattern `Campaign | Action | Detail` ("Stratos | Increase daily budget | to $150"). For anything else, a verb phrase a stranger understands: *"Fix checkout pixel double-firing"*, never *"pixel"*.
- **Client** — pick it, or leave internal. **Client sees it** stays ticked for real client deliverables (the client watches the card cross their own board from day one).
- **Project** — if this work belongs to a named outcome (Phase One!), pick it. Managers see this row; it's how the project page's numbers stay true.
- **Department + Lead** — see §3. If you can't name people, route to a **department** — its leads triage the queue.
- **Dates** — *Starts* is when work begins, *Due* is the promise. **Every client commitment gets a due date.** No date = it will never be chased.
- **Estimate** — minutes of expected effort. Optional; the service template fills a default. Managers use it for capacity, so a rough number beats a blank.
- **Description / steps** — enough that the assignee needs zero follow-up questions: the ask, the acceptance criteria ("done when…"), links to assets.

**Specialists:** anything that comes up during your day — a bug you spotted, a client email that needs an action — you file it yourself the moment it appears (quick-add title is enough, tidy it later). Work that isn't a card doesn't exist.

## 2. What kind of task to create — the catalogue

| Situation | Create | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A client commitment from a meeting/email | 1 card per **deliverable**, via ✦ Plan with AI | Due date = the promise. Share with client ON. |
| A deliverable with distinct phases (brief → produce → QA) | **1 card + steps**, each step owned | Not three cards — the deliverable is one thing. See §4. |
| Work that needs two departments to each deliver something (e.g. Creative makes the video, IT builds the landing page) | **2 cards**, one per department, second one **parked "waiting on another task"** if it can't start first | Different accountable leads = different cards. |
| A weekly/monthly retainer deliverable | **Recurring service** (AM sets it once) | The system mints the card each period — never re-type it. |
| Something you noticed that someone should do someday | Card in **To Do**, no assignee, routed to the right department, priority Low | The queue is the team's shared inbox. |
| A blocker on existing work | **Don't create a task** — park the existing card with the right reason (§6) | A blocker is a state, not new work. |
| "True when Phase One is done" statements | **Milestone** on the project | Tick it when it's true; never assign it. |

**Anti-patterns (don't):** one mega-card called "September marketing" (unmeasurable — split by deliverable); a card per 10-minute step (drowns the board — use steps); duplicating a client's Atrium card into a Sentinel card by hand (use the existing bridge/adoption); tasks with no client AND no department AND no assignee (invisible to everyone — route it somewhere).

## 3. Who's on a card — lead, support, step owners

- **Lead (assignee)** — exactly ONE. The accountable neck. "Who owns this?" always has a one-name answer.
- **Support** — any number of helpers. They see and can edit the card, it counts on their workload, but accountability stays with the lead. **Two people building one deliverable together = 1 lead + 1 support**, never two cards for the same thing.
- **Step owners** — inside the breakdown, each phase/step can be owned by someone. This is how a card crosses departments in sequence *within one deliverable*: Earl owns "Analyze", Leo owns "Write findings". A step named to you puts the card on your board and shows "N steps on you".
- Naming anyone but yourself — as lead, support or on a step — is **delegation**: team lead and up. Specialists can always claim unowned work, add themselves, or route to a department queue.

## 4. Steps (subtasks) — shaping the inside of a card

Open the card → **Work** tab:

- **Add main task** = a phase ("Analyze performance"); each phase holds steps ("Pull last 90 days", "Segment by placement").
- Give each step an **owner** if more than one person works the card. Only a step's owner (or the card's lead, or a manager) can tick it done — "done" is a claim about work, and only the person who did it gets to make it.
- **Apply a service template** to seed the standard breakdown for standard work (audits, reports, launches) — then edit. Every client deliverable of a known type should start from its template so nothing is forgotten.
- The progress bar = ticked steps / total steps. Keep steps honest: a step is real work, not decoration.

## 5. Working a card — the daily loop (specialists)

1. Clock in → **Today** shows your work in priority order.
2. Open the top card → **Start Work** (this moves it to In Progress and starts your task timer — time is measured by the button, never typed).
3. Finished, or switching? **Pause** (starting another card pauses this one automatically; clock-out closes everything).
4. Deliverable ready → attach/link the output → **Submit for review**. Your reviewer (team lead or the card's AM) approves or requests changes with written feedback. **Only approved work enters Completed** — this is the one enforced gate on the board.
5. Stuck? **Park it with the real reason** (§6) the moment you're stuck — not at 6pm.

## 6. Parking (blockers) — say WHY, structurally

Parking asks for a **kind** — pick honestly, it drives who chases what:

- **Waiting on the client** → shows on the AM's screen and ages on the client's health.
- **Waiting for access / an asset / a reviewer / an AM decision / another task / other** → these are *on us*, and the Operations page counts them against us, not the client.

Plus one plain sentence ("Sent the access request to Dana 9/2, waiting on IT contact"). A parked card without a real reason is worse than an honest overdue one.

## 7. Projects — tracking Phase One (COO / super admin)

**Projects page → Phase One.** It answers, in one screen: the goal, which milestones are done (ticked, stamped, by whom), what work is linked, what's late, and a health colour **with the reason in words** (the rule is printed on the page).

- **Milestones** = the definition of done for the phase. Tick one only when it is *true* — the tick is audited.
- **Linking work**: create tasks *into* the project ("✦ Plan work with AI" on the project page pre-links them; the New Task form has a Project row), so the rollup stays automatic. An unlinked card is invisible to the project — when in doubt, link it.
- One glance protocol for the COO each morning: Projects (is the phase on track?) → Operations (what's exceptional today?) → done.
- Keep projects RARE — a handful of named outcomes, not a folder per client. Client work is already tracked per client.

## 8. Roles — who does what (and the super admin vs team lead question, answered)

| | Creates tasks | Assigns people | Sets priority | Reviews | Projects | Sees |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Specialist** | own + routes to queues | self only | — | — | — | own work + own department |
| **Team Lead** (= Department Head) | yes | within what they can see | yes | **yes** | view | their department(s) |
| **Account Manager** | yes (AI-first) | anywhere | yes | yes | **manage** | all clients + board |
| **Admin / COO** | yes | anywhere | yes | yes | manage | everything operational |
| **Super Admin** (CEO/owner) | yes | anywhere | yes | yes | manage | everything + platform |

**Do we need both Super Admin and Team Lead? Yes — they are different jobs, not different sizes of the same job.** A Team Lead is a *department head*: they own craft, QA and their people's work — inside their department. A Super Admin owns the *platform*: payroll, permissions, roles, deleting people, acting as users, the Manage console. Merging them would hand every department head payroll and the power to grant themselves anything (the permission system locks role-setting to Super Admin for exactly this reason). What CAN look similar day-to-day (both approve reviews, both assign) is deliberate overlap of *capabilities*, and capabilities are tunable per role or per person in **Admin → Permissions** without touching the role ladder. Keep: **Super Admin = Ian + Zhen only. Team Lead = each department head. They coexist.**
